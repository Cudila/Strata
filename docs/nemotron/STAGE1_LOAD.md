## Correctness tests

1. **The NVFP4 format** (`test_nvfp4_format.py`, the experts of layer 1 against the BF16 checkpoint).
   - Decoding `e2m1(nibble) × e4m3(scale[row, col/16]) × scale_2`, with the low nibble as the even element, gives a
     relative error of **0.0813** against BF16. That is the NVFP4 quantization itself.
   - With the nibbles swapped the error is 1.41, without `scale_2` 4.75e4. So the test tells wrong readings apart.
   - Repacking into ggml's `block_nvfp4` is lossless (0.0).
   - **No deviations from the task's description.** Additions: a static `input_scale` (W4A4 in vLLM). ModelOpt picks a
     block's scale by minimum MSE among the neighbouring E4M3 codes (99.2% match), not by rounding amax/6 (32%).
2. **Our NVFP4 encoder** (for FP8 → NVFP4) uses the same scale search. On the experts it gives an error of 0.0812
   against 0.0813 for ModelOpt, 99.0–99.7% matching scales and 90% matching bytes (the rest are ties in rounding).
3. **Loading in C++** (`strata-nemotron-check`) on both GGUFs: PASS.
   - The geometry equals `config.json`: 88 blocks + 1 MTP, the order `MEMEMEM*E…`, 40/40/8, all sizes, top-22, scale 5,
     norm.
   - All 1109 / 1222 tensors are accounted for: name, type, exact shape, file bounds, and every NVFP4 tensor has its
     `.scale`. No extra tensors in the file.
4. **Strata's shared code.** In `gguf_reader.hpp`, numeric metadata arrays of up to 4096 items are kept whole (it used
   to keep the first 64). No reader in Strata uses `items` except the new code. `gguf_reader_test` passes, and the
   swift15 GGUF reads fine. swift15's logits and speed do not change by construction (the qwen4exp path does not read
   these fields). The full swift15 A/B was done at the first change to shared kernels.

## Found along the way

- **llama.cpp's converter and this checkpoint.** Four problems, all worked around in `convert.py` without patching
  the vendored code:
  1. the vision tensors are not dropped;
  2. the NVFP4 MTP heads are packed before the `mtp.*` rename, which gives "Can not map tensor";
  3. the expert count is looked up as `num_local_experts`, but Nemotron has `n_routed_experts`. Without the fix, all
     59 GiB of experts pile up in RAM (OOM at 110 GB);
  4. `quant_method: modelopt_mixed` does not reach the FP8 dequantization branch.

  Peak conversion memory is ~65 GB (GGUFWriter holds all packed arrays until it writes). Time: ~5 min per variant.
- **llama.cpp does not load NVFP4 inside the MTP block.** It does not create the per-tensor `.scale` of block 88's
  experts. That is why ggml-org ships the MTP as a separate Q4_0 file made from BF16. For the end-to-end llama.cpp
  reference we used the `--no-mtp` variants.
- **The Mamba reference.** transformers' chunked path in fp32 differs from the exact recurrence by 2e-3, up to 6e-3 by
  token 28, and the difference grows along the sequence. transformers' recurrent path matches the naive fp64. The
  reference for the stage 2 kernels is `mamba_naive.py` (fp64), not chunked transformers.
- **FP8 → NVFP4 at the weight level.** The relative error is 0.080–0.084 on every class (in_proj, out_proj, q/k/v/o,
  lm_head). That is the same as for the official NVFP4 experts. The model-level result is the KL below.

## FP8 → NVFP4: KL over the model

`llama-perplexity` (llama.cpp, CUDA), wikitext-2 test, 16 × 1024 tokens, experts on the CPU (`-ot exps=CPU`). The
baseline is `fp8bf16-nomtp`: FP8 expanded exactly to BF16, so its values are the checkpoint's. The experts are the
same NVFP4 in every variant.

| dense FP8 tensors (in/out_proj, q/k/v/o, lm_head) as | dense, GiB | PPL | PPL / baseline | mean KL | KL p99 | top-1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BF16 (baseline) against itself: the measurement floor | 11.57 | 3.0214 | 1.0018 | 0.000000 | 0.00004 | 100.0% |
| Q8_0 (`--fp8-as-q8`) | ~6.2 | — | 1.0000 ± 0.0030 | 0.0252 ± 0.0009 | 0.366 | 95.0% |
| NVFP4 (our encoder, ModelOpt-equivalent) | 4.60 | 3.1034 | 1.0290 ± 0.0049 | **0.0714 ± 0.0018** | 0.771 | 90.9% |

For scale: AtomicChat measures KL 0.025 for full NVFP4 builds (experts and everything else) against BF16, and 0.013
for AD-IQ4_NL. So moving only the dense FP8 layers (~6% of the weights) to NVFP4 adds almost three times as much as
NVFP4 on all the experts. Even Q8_0 for these layers costs KL 0.025 at an unchanged perplexity. The model is sensitive
precisely to them.

**Per class** (only this class in NVFP4, the other FP8 tensors exactly in BF16):

| in NVFP4 | saved against FP8 | PPL / baseline | mean KL | top-1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mamba in_proj (40 × [18560, 4096]) | 1.24 GiB | 1.0090 ± 0.0037 | 0.0400 | 93.6% |
| Mamba out_proj (40 × [4096, 8192]) | 0.55 GiB | 1.0054 ± 0.0035 | 0.0361 | 93.7% |
| attention q/k/v/o (8 blocks) | 0.12 GiB | 1.0036 ± 0.0033 | 0.0314 | 94.2% |
| lm_head | 0.22 GiB | 1.0128 ± 0.0024 | 0.0140 | 95.4% |
| all together | 2.13 GiB | 1.0290 ± 0.0049 | 0.0714 | 90.9% |
| all in Q8_0 | −0.30 GiB (larger than FP8) | 1.0000 ± 0.0030 | 0.0252 | 95.0% |

KL does not add up. The tiny attention tensors (0.27 GiB) alone give 0.031, and all of the dense layers in Q8_0 give
0.025 at an unchanged perplexity. A likely explanation, not checked separately: any small perturbation flips nearly
tied top-22 choices in the router, and that alone gives KL ~0.02–0.03.

A consequence for stage 7: a Strata engine whose arithmetic differs from llama.cpp's will give KL ~0.02 against
llama.cpp even when fully correct. Compare perplexity, top-1 and KL together, and measure the "floor" the same way,
by switching to arithmetic known to be equivalent.

Perplexity separates the classes: lm_head costs the most (+1.3% for 0.22 GiB), then in_proj (+0.9%).

**Conclusion.** NVFP4 for the FP8 layers costs +2.9% perplexity for 2.13 GiB. That is ≈ 740 experts, 3.6% of the
20,480, in VRAM. Q8_0 gives the same perplexity as FP8 (1.0000) and takes only 0.30 GiB more than FP8. A native FP8
type in the pack would win just those 0.30 GiB, at the cost of a new kernel.

**Decision** (the user: "the goal is speed, if the perplexity is acceptable"). A mix: Mamba in_proj/out_proj in NVFP4,
attention and lm_head in Q8_0:
- built with `convert.py --fp8 nvfp4 --nvfp4-only "mixer\.(in|out)_proj" --fp8-as-q8`;
- this is the published file, `Nemotron-3.5-Super-120B-A12B-NVFP4`: 1189 tensors, check PASS;
- measured (`mix-nomtp`): **PPL / baseline 1.0164 ± 0.0041**, KL 0.048, top-1 92.7%; dense 4.98 GiB;
- against Q8_0 for everything (~7.0 GiB dense), that is −2.0 GiB of VRAM and −2.0 GiB read per verify-window pass;
- full NVFP4 would save another 0.4 GiB for +1.3% PPL on top.
